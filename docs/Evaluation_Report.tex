\section{Setup}

Everything here comes from one run of \texttt{src/pipeline.py}: 8 target words,
1,092 SemCor occurrences, a linear SVM and multinomial Naive Bayes, and window
sizes $W = \pm 2$ and $W = \pm 5$. Scores are from stratified 5-fold
cross-validation, so each occurrence is predicted once by a model that did not
train on it. The Architecture and Methodology document covers the features and
the protocol in detail.

\section{Window size impact}

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/window_f1.pdf}
\caption{Macro F1 per word at both window sizes.}
\label{fig:window}
\end{figure}

A wider window does not help overall. SVM macro F1 drops from 0.54 to 0.50,
mostly on \emph{interest}, \emph{plant}, \emph{power} and \emph{rate}, and Naive
Bayes stays about flat. The useful cues for these words are local (``interest
rate'', ``power plant''), and $\pm 5$ adds two to three times as many features,
mostly rare words that act as noise at this data size.

\emph{bank} is the exception (SVM accuracy 0.68 to 0.79). Its senses depend on
topic, so words like ``directors'' and ``approval'' a few positions away help
where the $\pm 2$ window only sees ``of the'' and ``with the''.

\section{SVM vs Naive Bayes}

The SVM is ahead on accuracy almost everywhere (only \emph{case} at $\pm 5$
goes to Naive Bayes, 0.56 vs 0.55), but the real gap is macro F1: 0.54 vs 0.40
at $\pm 2$. On words with many senses Naive Bayes mostly predicts the majority
sense (macro F1 of 0.16 on \emph{run}), likely because its independence
assumption double counts the overlapping POS and word features.

\paragraph{Too little context.} Many factory/organism errors are occurrences
like ``into each hole with the \textbf{plant}'', where the $\pm 2$ window has
no content words and the model falls back to the majority sense.

\paragraph{Rare senses get outvoted.} 5 of the 8 verb uses of \emph{plant} are
predicted as a noun sense despite the verb POS tag. With about six verb examples
per fold, the target POS does not carry enough weight. \emph{interest.v.01} has
the same problem (4 of 5 wrong).

\paragraph{Fine-grained senses.} Some distinctions are hard even for people:
``insurance \textbf{rates}'' is gold \emph{rate.n.02} (an amount charged) but
predicted as \emph{rate.n.01} (a magnitude). The financial sense of
\emph{interest} (\emph{interest.n.04}), the one that matters most for news, is
right only 2 times out of 14 and is usually mistaken for \emph{interest.n.01}
(curiosity).

\section{Limitations}

\begin{itemize}
\item SemCor is written text. Real transcripts have no punctuation or casing and
contain recognition errors, which will hurt the POS tagger and through it the
POS features. Expect lower numbers on actual spoken news.
\item The data is small. \emph{bank} has only 34 usable occurrences, so a
difference of one prediction moves its accuracy by about 0.03.
\item Both classifiers use default hyperparameters, and results are from a
single cross-validation seed.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}

A linear SVM over target POS, neighbouring POS and a $\pm 2$ bag-of-words works
best here, with 0.65 accuracy and 0.54 macro F1 overall. Widening the window to
$\pm 5$ only pays off for words whose senses are topical, like \emph{bank}; for
the rest it adds noise. Naive Bayes is close on accuracy but much worse on
rare senses. The main sources of error are short, uninformative windows,
senses with very few examples, and WordNet distinctions that are finer than the
context can resolve.
