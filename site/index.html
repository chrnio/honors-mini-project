Supervised WSD for Spoken News
Problem Approach Demo & Results

Problem Statement

News broadcasts are full of words whose meaning shifts with context. bank can be a riverbank or a financial institution. interest can mean curiosity or a loan charge. plant can be a factory or something you grow in soil. A transcript‑processing system that cannot tell these apart gets the underlying facts wrong, even when it transcribed every word correctly.

This project asks a narrow, answerable version of that question: for eight frequently ambiguous words (bankinterestplantrunstatecaseratepower), can a classifier learn which dictionary sense is meant in a sentence, using nothing but the word's part of speech and the words sitting near it?

Step by Step Explanation

1
Start from sentences with known answers
SemCor is a corpus where every word is already tagged with its correct dictionary sense (from WordNet). Every sentence containing one of the eight target words becomes one labelled example.
2
Look only at the neighbours
For each occurrence, take a window of nearby words — either 2 or 5 words on each side — and record their part‑of‑speech tags plus a simple word count (bag‑of‑words). The target word itself is never shown its own meaning, only its surroundings.
3
Turn that into numbers
Those tags and word counts become a feature vector — a row of numbers a classifier can actually learn from, built fresh for every single occurrence of every target word.
4
Train two different classifiers per word
A linear Support Vector Machine and a Naive Bayes model are each trained separately for every target word, since "bank" and "plant" have entirely different sets of possible senses.
5
Test fairly, then compare
5‑fold cross‑validation splits the examples into 5 groups, training on 4 and testing on the 1 left out, rotating until every example has been tested once — so no model is ever graded on sentences it already memorised. Accuracy and macro F1 are then compared across both classifiers and both window sizes.
